I.  Introduction

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Somewhere, where I lead the Foundations and Systems Research Group. Before joining Somewhere in 2023, I completed my Ph.D. at Another University under the supervision of Prof. M. Advisor, and held a postdoctoral position at a Major Industry Research Lab.

My research asks a deceptively simple question: how do we build software systems whose behavior we can predict, explain, and trust at scale? I approach this question from three angles — language design, systems engineering, and empirical evaluation — and I believe progress requires moving fluidly among them.

This document is structured as follows. Section II describes my current research interests in more detail. Section III lists selected publications. Section IV summarizes teaching and student supervision. Section V provides contact information.

II.  Research Interests

A.  Type systems for numerical programs

Modern numerical code — from physics simulations to neural networks — routinely loses meaningful structure at the type level. My work develops refinement and dependent type systems that recover invariants such as tensor shape, unit, and differentiability, and that can be checked with predictable performance [1], [2].

B.  Latency-aware scheduling

Heterogeneous accelerators have made scheduling a first-class concern. I am interested in schedulers that expose tail-latency guarantees as a contract, and in compiler techniques that let high-level frameworks emit code that respects those contracts [3].

C.  Verified machine-learning compilers

A growing body of ML infrastructure is written without proof or even test of semantic correctness. With my students I am building a mechanized account of tensor algebra rewrites, with the goal of certifying optimizations used in production frameworks [4].
